What a business should review before signing a commercial contract

A plain-language framework covering scope, payment, liability, termination, confidentiality, IP, dispute resolution and renewal.

Practical business guide3 min readLegal

Direct answer

What is the practical takeaway?

Before signing a commercial contract, a business should understand exactly what it is agreeing to deliver, receive or pay for, and what happens if circumstances change. The review should cover scope, payment, liability, termination, confidentiality, intellectual property, dispute resolution and renewal provisions.

Key takeaways

The points worth carrying into the next decision.

  • Make the scope and responsibilities explicit.
  • Review payment terms and dependencies carefully.
  • Understand liability, indemnity and limitation provisions.
  • Check termination rights and post-termination obligations.
  • Confirm confidentiality and intellectual-property ownership.
  • Understand dispute-resolution and renewal mechanisms.

Start with the scope of work

The contract should make the actual commercial arrangement understandable. Ambiguous scope can create disagreements later because the parties may interpret the expected deliverables differently.

Review descriptions of services, products, milestones, responsibilities, exclusions and dependencies together rather than reading them in isolation.

Review payment and commercial terms

Payment terms define more than the final amount. The review should consider when payment becomes due, what events trigger invoicing, whether approvals are required and what happens when a dependency delays payment.

Commercial terms should be understood alongside delivery obligations so the business can assess the practical relationship between effort and payment.

Understand liability and indemnity

Liability provisions describe financial responsibility when something goes wrong. Indemnity provisions can create additional obligations depending on the wording of the agreement.

These clauses deserve careful review because their effect can extend beyond the normal description of the commercial scope.

Check termination and exit provisions

A contract should be reviewed for how the relationship can end, who can terminate it, what notice is required and which obligations survive termination.

Exit terms matter because the practical relationship between the parties can continue in some respects even after active delivery stops.

Review confidentiality and intellectual property

Commercial relationships often involve access to business information, designs, software, documentation, customer information or other materials. The contract should clearly describe what is protected and which rights belong to which party.

Intellectual-property language is particularly important where the work creates new content, software, designs, documentation or other deliverables.

Do not overlook disputes and renewal

The agreement should explain how disputes are handled and where applicable proceedings or resolution mechanisms occur. Renewal provisions should also be reviewed so the business understands whether the agreement expires, renews automatically or requires a fresh decision.

These provisions are easy to overlook because they may not affect the day-to-day delivery of the contract.

Frequently asked questions

Questions businesses commonly ask about this topic.

What clauses should a business review before signing a contract?

At minimum, review scope, payment, responsibilities, liability, indemnity, termination, confidentiality, intellectual property, dispute resolution and renewal provisions.

Why is scope important in a commercial contract?

A clear scope reduces ambiguity around what each party must deliver, provide, approve or pay for.

Should every commercial contract be reviewed by a legal professional?

The appropriate level of legal review depends on the contract, value, risk and circumstances. Contracts containing material legal or commercial exposure should be reviewed carefully before signing.
