IT Service — common questions

Direct answers about IT Service and the way it fits within the Ashwamedh Group business ecosystem.

What does the IT Service cover?

Ashwamedh's IT Service covers custom software, ERP and internal systems, web and mobile applications, cloud infrastructure, DevOps, cybersecurity audits, data and integration work, and growth marketing.

Who is the IT Service designed for?

The service is designed for growing businesses, operationally complex enterprises and technology-led companies that need software, infrastructure, security, data or digital execution support.

Can Ashwamedh build custom business software?

Yes. The service includes custom business applications, internal tools, workflow automation and ERP modules designed around the organisation's operating requirements.

Can you work with an existing codebase?

Yes. The service model includes technical audits, architecture review, dependency and security assessment, stabilisation, enhancement and ongoing support for existing codebases.

Does the IT Service include cloud and DevOps work?

Yes. The stated scope includes infrastructure provisioning, CI/CD pipelines, monitoring, backups and scaling-oriented cloud and DevOps support.

Does the IT Service include cybersecurity?

Yes. The service includes vulnerability assessment, penetration testing, access review, remediation guidance and security review before production release.

Who owns the software and cloud environment?

The service positioning states that the client organisation keeps the source repository, cloud accounts and technical documentation, supporting long-term ownership of the resulting system.

Can IT requirements be coordinated with hiring and finance?

Yes. Engineering capacity can connect with the Hiring Agency, while technology capital-expenditure requirements can connect with the Finance Service when needed.

Engineering approach

Most software projects fail on delivery discipline, not technology

Scope drifts, decisions stall, and six months later there is a demo that never reaches production. We work in short sprints with a running build you can open at any point.

Architecture is chosen for what you actually need to operate and maintain, not to demonstrate technical ambition. You keep the repository, the cloud accounts and the documentation from day one.

Execution over specification

Every engagement produces working, deployed software at regular checkpoints. Progress is something you can use, not something you are told about.

Delivery model

Sprint-based execution with visible checkpoints

You see working software early and often. Surprises get caught in week two, not month six.

  1. 01

    Discovery and scope

    Workshops to map users, workflows and constraints, ending in a prioritised backlog and a delivery estimate.

  2. 02

    Architecture and design

    System design, data model, integration plan and interface prototypes signed off before build begins.

  3. 03

    Sprint delivery

    Two-week cycles, each closing with a deployed build, a demo and a re-prioritised backlog.

  4. 04

    Hardening and security

    Load testing, security review, access control verification and remediation before production release.

  5. 05

    Launch and support

    Go-live, monitoring, documented handover and an agreed support window with response commitments.
